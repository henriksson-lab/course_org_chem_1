Synthesis and evidence: standalone problem bank

This bank supplies 20 non-R chemistry problems, with hints and worked explanations. Work through 1–10 for core practice,11–16 for transfer and 17–20 for deeper checks. Allow roughly 2–3 hours across the unit, taking longer where you draw a complete mechanism. Use the hints after a first attempt and the worked checks after revising. All required definitions, structures, constants and observations are supplied here or in the linked local explanations; no textbook problem set is required.

Working refresher

Keep these calculations distinct: amount n=m/M; reaction extent available from a reagent is n divided by its balanced coefficient; maximum product amount is limiting extent times the product coefficient.1 mol=1000 mmol. Theoretical product mass is product amount×product molar mass. Apparent isolated yield=100×isolated mass/theoretical product mass, interpreted as product yield only if the weighed mass is product. Yield does not measure purity or conversion. For TLC, Rf=spot-centre distance/front distance, both from the same origin; the ratio is dimensionless and depends on conditions. All masses/plates here are teaching data rather than a protocol. The lecture and practical report guide supply worked examples.

1. Plan a two-step functional-group route

Starting with ethanol, propose a route through ethanoic acid to ethyl ethanoate. Identify the separate ethanol portion needed in the second step and name each transformation.

Hint

The acid-derived and alcohol-derived fragments need not come from the same reacting molecule.

Worked explanation

Oxidise one ethanol portion to ethanoic acid, then use a separate ethanol portion in esterification. The first change is oxidation and the second acyl substitution. Conditions and work-up require suitable choices; this is a planning example, not a protocol.

2. Choose an oxidation endpoint

Ethanol can give ethanal and then ethanoic acid under appropriate oxidation conditions. Explain why a target labelled ethanal needs more information than an unspecified [O] arrow.

Hint

Further oxidation is a possible competing transformation.

Worked explanation

Conditions must favour forming and isolating the aldehyde rather than proceeding to acid. A target name does not guarantee selectivity or purity, and product evidence is still needed after the reaction.

3. Convert mass with visible units

A teaching amount table gives 1.3812 g salicylic acid and M=138.12 g/mol. Calculate mol and mmol, showing unit cancellation.

Hint

Divide mass by molar mass;1 mol=1000 mmol.

Worked explanation

1.3812 g/(138.12 g/mol)=0.01000 mol=10.00 mmol. Grams cancel. Converting mmol into mol requires dividing by 1000, which matters before multiplying by a molar mass in g/mol.

4. Compare a second reagent

For 1.53135 g acetic anhydride at 102.09 g/mol, calculate amount. In a 1:1 reaction with 10.00 mmol acid, identify the limiting reagent and justify without comparing masses.

Hint

The balanced reaction has coefficients one for both starting reagents.

Worked explanation

Anhydride amount is 0.01500 mol=15.00 mmol. Acid has the smaller n/coefficient,10/1 versus 15/1, and limits theoretical product. A larger mass alone would not establish excess.

5. Handle coefficients explicitly

For the illustrative equation 2A+B→P, starting amounts are 10 mmol A and 8 mmol B. Find limiting reagent and maximum P amount; explain why simply taking min(10,8) is wrong.

Hint

Compare n divided by reactant coefficient.

Worked explanation

A supplies 10/2=5 mmol reaction extent while B supplies 8/1=8 mmol. A limits and at most 5 mmol P forms. Taking the minimum raw amount ignores that two A units are required per product.

6. Include a product coefficient

For 2A+B→2P with 10 mmol A and 8 mmol B, find maximum P amount. Compare with the previous equation 2A+B→P using the same inputs.

Hint

The limiting extent is multiplied by the product coefficient.

Worked explanation

A still limits extent to 5 mmol. With product coefficient 2, maximum P=10 mmol; with coefficient 1 it is 5 mmol. Extent and product amount are not universally the same number.

7. Change the limiting reagent

In the 1:1 aspirin teaching reaction, acid stays 10.00 mmol but anhydride mass is 0.51045 g at 102.09 g/mol. Identify the new limiter and predict product mol and mass with M=180.16 g/mol.

Hint

Recompute the changed amount before reusing the original limiter label.

Worked explanation

Anhydride is 0.00500 mol=5.00 mmol and now limits. Theoretical aspirin is 0.00500 mol, giving 0.9008 g. The original 10 mmol acid is now in excess for the desired reaction.

8. Calculate theoretical mass

A1:1 teaching reaction has 10.0 mmol limiting reagent and product M=180.16 g/mol. Calculate the theoretical mass and state what assumptions the upper bound contains.

Hint

Convert 10.0 mmol to 0.0100 mol before multiplying.

Worked explanation

Theoretical mass is 1.8016 g before final rounding. It assumes the balanced desired transformation and complete use of limiting reagent toward that product; it does not establish actual conversion, recovery or purity.

9. Calculate apparent isolated yield

For theoretical mass 1.8016 g and isolated material 1.20 g, calculate 100×isolated/theoretical. State the chemical assumption behind calling it product yield.

Hint

The numerator must represent product mass for the interpretation to hold.

Worked explanation

The apparent isolated yield is 66.6%. Interpreting this as product yield assumes the weighed material is product with appropriate identity/purity; solvent or impurities can inflate its mass.

10. Predict a changed numerator

Theoretical mass remains 1.8016 g. Compare isolated masses 1.10 g and 1.20 g. Calculate both apparent yields and say which upstream quantities should remain unchanged.

Hint

Only isolated mass changes in the numerator.

Worked explanation

The yields are 61.1% and 66.6%. Starting-reagent amounts, limiting extent and theoretical mass should stay fixed. A report that changes those without changing the inputs has a dependency error.

11. Critique a yield above 100 percent

Theoretical mass is 1.8016 g but a sample weighs 1.95 g. Calculate the apparent yield and name two plausible classes of explanation without diagnosing a unique cause.

Hint

An apparent yield can expose a questionable mass or model assumption.

Worked explanation

The value is about 108.2%. Residual solvent/water or other impurities can add mass; a weighing, input or stoichiometry error is another possibility. It is not evidence of creating extra product atoms beyond the balanced maximum.

12. Separate yield and purity

One isolate has low apparent yield but the supplied spectra are consistent with the target; another has high apparent yield and major extra NMR signals. Explain why yield cannot rank their purity.

Hint

Yield measures recovered amount relative to a theoretical amount, not composition.

Worked explanation

A small recovered amount can be relatively pure, while a large mass can include impurities. Purity needs composition evidence; yield and identity/purity are different claims. The facts do not give exact purities of either sample.

13. Compute two Rf values

A TLC origin is 0 cm, solvent front 5.0 cm, starting-material spot 1.2 cm and product-reference spot 3.2 cm. Calculate both Rf values and give units.

Hint

Rf=distance from origin to spot centre divided by distance from origin to solvent front.

Worked explanation

The values are 0.24 and 0.64, dimensionless. Both distances must use the same baseline and units; the numbers are conditional on stationary phase, eluent and other conditions.

14. Read a crude TLC lane

Under the same conditions, a crude lane has spots at 1.2 cm and 3.2 cm with front 5.0 cm; reference lanes match those positions. Write a restrained interpretation and one limitation.

Hint

Matching retention supports comparison but is not a unique molecular fingerprint.

Worked explanation

At least two detected components are present, with retention consistent with the references. Co-migration or invisible components remain possible; spot matching alone does not uniquely establish identity or quantitative conversion.

15. Recognise missing information

A TLC image records a spot 3.0 cm above baseline but no solvent-front position. Can Rf be calculated? What observation is missing, and why should a typical front distance not be invented?

Hint

A ratio needs both numerator and denominator.

Worked explanation

Rf cannot be determined reliably. The actual front travel from the same baseline is missing. A presumed usual distance would substitute an invented observation and could give a false retention value.

16. Test the single-spot purity claim

A and B each run at Rf 0.64 in one eluent, and their mixture shows one spot. Explain how this can happen and propose a useful follow-up kind of evidence.

Hint

The separation method may fail to resolve different compounds.

Worked explanation

Co-migration can give one retention feature for a mixture. A suitable changed separation condition or independent spectral assessment can test composition. One spot is not a guarantee of purity, and detection can miss some components.

17. Track the aspirin functional-group change

Salicylic acid has adjacent aromatic COOH and OH groups. In the teaching aspirin target, the phenolic oxygen becomes O–C(=O)CH₃ while COOH remains. State which group changes and why acid O–H evidence may persist.

Hint

Not every oxygen-containing group is consumed by phenolic acetylation.

Worked explanation

Phenolic OH is converted to an acetate ester. The carboxylic acid remains, so acid-region features can persist in the target. Acid O–H alone does not uniquely establish unreacted salicylic acid.

18. Interpret an extra NMR signal

An isolate has a plausible mass-based yield and one TLC spot but an extra major NMR signal group. Give two possibilities and a targeted next check without naming a definite impurity.

Hint

The identity/purity assumption should be tested against independent evidence.

Worked explanation

Residual solvent or another component, including starting material, could contribute; assignment error is also possible. Compare full integrations/assignments and suitable references or use a discriminating separation. Do not discard contradictory spectra because yield looks plausible.

19. Audit a report dependency

A student changes isolated mass from 1.20 g to 1.10 g and the report also changes theoretical product mol from 0.0100 to 0.0090. Starting-reagent masses were unchanged. Identify the error and the correct dependency.

Hint

The theoretical maximum comes from starting materials, not from recovered mass.

Worked explanation

Theoretical mol must remain 0.0100 under the unchanged reaction inputs. Isolated mass only changes the apparent yield numerator. Reconstruct calculations from saved raw inputs and keep the independent measurements separate.

20. Write a supported conclusion

Supplied teaching evidence gives theoretical mass 1.8016 g, isolate 1.20 g, crude TLC two spots and a product-like simulated IR comparison. Write a conclusion separating amount, reaction evidence and remaining identity/purity limits.

Hint

Avoid allowing one observation to answer every question.

Worked explanation

Apparent isolated yield is 66.6% under the product-mass assumption. Crude TLC shows at least two detected components, and the labelled teaching IR illustrates consistent functional groups. These observations do not uniquely prove a real isolate’s identity or purity, especially when the trace is simulated.

R task: results that update when a mass changes

In the student project, save a results section containing the amount table, limiting-reagent reasoning, theoretical mass and isolated yield. Reuse code from the report guide or adapt this transparent calculation:

library(readr)
library(dplyr)
amounts <- read_csv("data/amounts.csv", show_col_types = FALSE) |>
  mutate(amount_mol = mass_g / molar_mass_g_mol,
         extent_mol = amount_mol / stoich)
theoretical_product_mol <- min(amounts$extent_mol)
theoretical_mass_g <- theoretical_product_mol * 180.16
isolated_mass_g <- 1.20
isolated_yield_percent <- 100 * isolated_mass_g / theoretical_mass_g
knitr::kable(amounts)
isolated_yield_percent

The amounts.csv table contains the two starting reagents. If adding product or solvent rows later, select the relevant starting reagents before min(). Inspect the table and check the units. Write the numerical yield using inline R in the report, not by typing a second independent copy of the number. Add your TLC table and an interpretation distinguishing evidence for reaction, identity and purity. Change the isolated mass to 1.10 g, predict the direction of change, and Knit again before restoring the original teaching value.

Reproducibility check

At 1.10 g the apparent isolated yield becomes about 61.1%. Every dependent result should change when knitted. If a copied number remains 66.6%, it is no longer linked to the saved calculation. Correctness also requires the right stoichiometry and trustworthy primary observations; automatic updating alone cannot supply either.

Finish with two sentences stating what the observations support and what remains uncertain. Optional reading: selected Solomons chapters 8, 12 and 17; see the reading guide.
