Day 9: Exercises — Models, rotations and configurations

Compare Newman and Fischer drawings, follow a chair flip, and distinguish conformation from configuration. Core 1–8: about 75–95 minutes including comparison and correction. Extension 9–12: about 30–45 minutes for optional independent work. Revisit the hints after an attempt; each worked check explains the rule, not just the label.

Refresher and worked starting point

No later reactions are needed. Newman:front dot, rear circle. Butane anti180°, gauche±60°; staggered sets offset60°. Ring flip swaps axial/equatorial and preserves up/down. CIP compares attached atomic numbers, then first difference. For lactic acid OH>COOH>CH₃>H. Fischer horizontal=toward, vertical=away. Worked start:group4 away with clockwise1→2→3 gives R; invert if4 points toward.

Local lessons: conformations, stereochemistry.

1. Specify an eye position

Draw butane, number C1–C4, and look C3→C2. List front/rear methyl labels.

Hint

Front is nearest your eye in the stated direction.

Worked check

C3 is front, with C4 methyl; C2 is rear, with C1 methyl. Each also bears two H. Reversing the view swaps front/rear but does not alter connectivity.

2. Build staggered anti

Along C2→C3, place front CH₃ at 12 and rear CH₃ at 6. Complete the four H positions.

Hint

Separate each carbon’s set by 120°.

Worked check

Front H at 4/8 and rear H at 2/10 make a staggered projection. The methyl dihedral is 180°, so it is anti.

3. Generate both gauche arrangements

Hold front methyl at 12. Draw rear methyl at 2 and 10, completing both projections.

Hint

The methyl groups are ±60° apart.

Worked check

Both are gauche and staggered. They preserve the butane framework and are equivalent in energy for ordinary butane in an achiral environment.

4. Repair a false isomer claim

A student calls anti and gauche constitutional isomers. Name the unchanged atom relationships.

Hint

Connectivity tests constitutional difference.

Worked check

Every carbon keeps the same neighbouring atoms. Bond rotation changes conformation, not constitution. The student confused spatial view with bonded framework.

5. Follow an up group

Carry axial-up methyl through a cyclohexane chair flip. Mark its side and local position afterward.

Hint

Use two separate labels.

Worked check

It becomes equatorial-up. Changing it to equatorial-down would alter the up/down configuration, not simply flip a chair.

6. Check cis/trans persistence

Two ring substituents start up/up. A third example has up/down. State each relation before/after a flip.

Hint

Same side is cis; opposite sides trans.

Worked check

Up/up is cis and stays cis. Up/down is trans and stays trans. Axial/equatorial labels may exchange in both.

7. Find a tetrahedral centre

Compare CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ and CH₃CH(OH)COOH. List groups and identify which qualifies.

Hint

One repeated group prevents this simple stereocentre.

Worked check

Propan-2-ol has two methyl groups and does not qualify. Lactic acid has OH, H, CH₃, COOH and qualifies.

8. Assign a complete Fischer

Draw COOH top, CH₃ bottom, OH left, H right. Assign S/R with priorities and the viewing correction.

Hint

H is horizontal and forward.

Worked check

PriorityOH1, COOH2, CH₃3, H4. The trace is clockwise but4 toward requires inversion, so S.

Optional extension

Use these after the core or during later revision.

9. Mirror without changing connectivity

Mirror the previous Fischer. Assign its descriptor independently and classify the pair.

Hint

Swap left/right groups while keeping the vertical labels.

Worked check

The mirror has OH right, H left and is R. The pair is enantiomeric, with matching connectivity and opposite one-centre configuration.

10. Classify an alkene pair

Draw same-side and opposite-side methyl groups around but-2-ene C=C. Assign E/Z and isomer relationship.

Hint

Methyl outranks H at both ends.

Worked check

Same-side is Z, opposite E. These are diastereomers, not conformers related by ordinary free single-bond rotation.

11. Test a nonexample

Can CH₂=C(CH₃)₂ have E/Z? Give the failed requirement at each end.

Hint

Inspect duplicated substituents rather than geometry alone.

Worked check

No. One end has H/H and the other CH₃/CH₃. Each alkene carbon must have two different substituents.

12. Separate centre count from chirality

Draw meso-tartaric-acid Fischer with COOH top/bottom, OH right at both centres. Explain its symmetry in words.

Hint

Inspect equivalent upper/lower halves.

Worked check

The complete structure has an internal symmetry relationship and is achiral despite two stereocentres. Counting centres alone does not establish chirality.

Predict first

With G(B) − G(A) = 3500 J/mol at 298.15 K, which state should be more populated? Would adding the same free-energy constant to both states change the fractions?

Explore

  1. Inspect the starting prediction and verify that fractions A and B sum to one. Compare the relative B/A weight with B’s normalised population fraction.

  2. Set equal-state free energies, then make B lower in free energy by moving ΔG below zero. Explain the reversal without assigning a new molecular identity to A or B.

  3. Hold the energy gap fixed and vary temperature. Then change only the common reference offset. Explain why the raw state weights can change while normalised fractions do not.

Take-away

Separate three quantities: relative free-energy difference, normalised equilibrium population and interconversion barrier. Which one is missing for a rate prediction?

Model and limits

This is a supplied two-effective-state equilibrium model; the state free energies already include degeneracy. Use consistent J/mol, R = 8.314462618 J/(mol K) and kelvin. A and B are abstract conformer states, not an asserted complete inventory of butane conformers. No activation barrier or rate constant is supplied.
