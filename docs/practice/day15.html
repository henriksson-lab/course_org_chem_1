Day 15: Exercises — Redox, amounts and evidence

Connect alcohol and carbonyl transformations to reagent amounts, isolated yield and experimental evidence. Revisit the alcohols and redox lecture, then explore the limiting-reagent and yield demo.

Core 1–8: about 80–100 minutes. Transfer 9–12: an optional 30–45 minutes. Draw before opening a check; compare reasoning with a partner and keep your own corrected drawing.

Refresher and worked start

Full-headed arrows move pairs from bonds/lone pairs; ordinary carbon has total bond order four. Alpha is the leaving-group carbon; beta is its neighbour. E2 uses an appropriate anti geometry and three linked pair movements. Selected carbonyl reduction gives alkoxide then alcohol after proton transfer.

For the worked starting example OH⁻+CH₃Br→CH₃OH+Br⁻, draw OH pair→C and C–Br pair→Br. Both sides have net charge −1; the concerted step contains no carbocation. Use the same atom/charge audit in the problems below.

Calculation conventions: n=m/M with g and g/mol gives mol;1000 mmol=1 mol. Compare n/coefficient for a balanced equation. Yield%=100×isolated/theoretical mass. TLC (thin-layer chromatography) Rf=spot/front distance using the same origin and matched plate conditions. All numerical observations below are constructed teaching data, not real experimental results.

For the local carbon oxidation-state comparison, assign +1 per C–O bond order, −1 per C–H bond and 0 per C–C bond. Thus the OH-bearing carbon of ethanol has +1 − 2 = −1, while the carbonyl carbon of ethanal has +2 − 1 = +1. These supplied rules are sufficient for the C/H/O examples below.

1. Map two directions

Draw ethanol→ethanal and ethanal→ethanol. Label oxidation and reduction and identify the changing carbon.

Hint

Compare local C–O bond orders and C–H bonds.

Worked answer and reasoning

Ethanol to ethanal is oxidation: the changing carbon rises from −1 to+1 in the supplied bond-count convention. Returning to ethanol is reduction. The same two-carbon skeleton is retained.

2. Extend a secondary-alcohol map

Draw butan-2-ol and predict its selected carbon-skeleton-retaining oxidation product. Explain why butanal is not the corresponding product.

Hint

The OH-bearing carbon remains connected to methyl and ethyl groups.

Worked answer and reasoning

The product is butan-2-one CH₃COCH₂CH₃. Butanal would place the carbonyl at an end, changing which carbon bears it rather than simply oxidising the original secondary centre.

3. Detect impossible valence

A student oxidises(CH₃)₃COH to (CH₃)₃C=O with all C–C bonds retained. Draw and count the central bond orders.

Hint

Three single C–C bonds plus a double C=O are too many.

Worked answer and reasoning

The proposed carbon has bond-order sum five. A tertiary alcohol lacks the carbinol C–H required for the simple alcohol-to-carbonyl map. Harsh C–C-cleaving oxidation is a different process.

4. Reduce then protonate

Use the selected NaBH₄-type model to reduce propanal, then use water for proton donation. Draw the alkoxide and neutral product.

Hint

Hydride delivery makes C–H while pi electrons go to oxygen.

Worked answer and reasoning

Propanal gives CH₃CH₂CH₂O⁻ first, then propan-1-ol plus OH⁻ after water protonation. H:⁻ is reagent-delivery shorthand, not free hydride persisting in water.

5. Prepare an amount table by hand

For the supplied1:1 aspirin teaching equation, calculate mmol from 1.3812 g salicylic acid at 138.12 g/mol and1.53135 g acetic anhydride at 102.09 g/mol.

Hint

Use n=m/M; mmol=1000×mol.

Worked answer and reasoning

The values are 10.00 mmol acid and15.00 mmol anhydride. Acid limits the 1:1 reaction to 10.00 mmol product. The equation is a calculation context rather than a laboratory protocol.

6. Change the limiting reagent

Keep the acid input but use0.51045 g anhydride at 102.09 g/mol. Predict the limit before calculating the maximum product mass at 180.16 g/mol.

Hint

The changed anhydride input is 5.00 mmol.

Worked answer and reasoning

Anhydride now limits product to 5.00 mmol. Theoretical mass=0.00500 mol×180.16 g/mol=0.9008 g. Reusing the original acid-based theoretical mass would overstate the new maximum.

7. Apply a nonunit coefficient

For the hypothetical balanced stoichiometry A+2B→P, amounts are 8 mmol A and12 mmol B. Calculate the maximum P.

Hint

Divide each amount by its coefficient.

Worked answer and reasoning

A permits 8 mmol extent; B permits 6 mmol. B limits the maximum P to 6 mmol. Raw amount alone would miss the stoichiometric demand.

8. Compute and interpret yield

The original aspirin teaching case has theoretical mass1.8016 g and isolated mass1.20 g. Calculate yield and explain why it does not give conversion.

Hint

Use 100×isolated/theoretical, then think about isolation loss.

Worked answer and reasoning

Isolated yield is about 66.6%. Product can be lost during separation even if substantial starting material reacted; side reactions and contamination also complicate the relation. Conversion needs evidence about starting-material consumption.

Note

Optional transfer problems 9–12. Select for revision; they are not additional required contact hours.

9. Keep every TLC spot

A matched normal-phase teaching plate has solvent front5.0 cm. Starting-material spot travels1.2 cm, reference product3.2 cm, and crude has both spots. Calculate each Rf and interpret the two crude spots.

Hint

Rf=spot distance/front distance; do not average two different compounds.

Worked answer and reasoning

The Rf values are 0.24 and0.64. Crude contains two resolved spots under these conditions, consistent with more than one detectable component. Co-migration supports comparison but does not prove identity; invisible impurities can remain.

10. Interpret a functional-group change

A proposed secondary-alcohol oxidation loses broad alcohol OH absorption and gains strong C=O absorption. What is supported, and what does one visible TLC spot fail to establish?

Hint

Separate functional class from complete identity and purity.

Worked answer and reasoning

The IR supports formation of a carbonyl-containing material and loss of the alcohol feature. A single TLC spot does not prove pure intended ketone; co-migration or undetected components and other carbonyl structures remain possible.

11. Check primary records

A report has a yield but no sample identifiers, no raw mass record and a spectrum labelled only unknown. Name three specific repairs and why each matters.

Hint

A later result needs to be tied to its actual observation.

Worked answer and reasoning

Restore sample IDs linking isolated material and spectrum; retain dated raw mass/amount records; label the spectrum axes, conditions and sample provenance. These make the chemical claim traceable instead of a disconnected number and picture.

12. Assemble an evidence paragraph

Write four sentences using these teaching observations:1.20 g isolated, theoretical1.8016 g, crude TLC two spots, isolated sample one visible spot under matched conditions, carbonyl IR present. Include a justified limit.

Hint

Report observations first, then the conclusion they actually support.

Worked answer and reasoning

A defensible paragraph reports66.6% apparent isolated mass yield, records that crude had two visible TLC components and isolated material one under the stated conditions, and notes carbonyl evidence. It explicitly avoids equating one spot with purity or carbonyl presence with unique identity; more structure evidence is needed.

Explore the chemistry

Prediction, exploration and explanation

Predict (1–2 minutes). The starting masses represent 10.00 mmol salicylic acid and 15.00 mmol acetic anhydride in a 1:1 reaction. Predict the maximum aspirin amount and mass before changing a control.

Explore (3–4 minutes). Use the anhydride-limited preset and explain the new limit. Restore the default, then test zero acid, an empty field, a negative input and an isolate mass above the theoretical maximum. Distinguish an invalid input from a valid zero measurement.

Explain (2 minutes). Report the default theoretical mass and isolated yield. Why is yield undefined when theoretical product is zero? Why should an apparent yield above 100% trigger a check of composition/inputs instead of being clamped to 100% or celebrated as extra product?

Supplied model and its limits

Supplied balanced teaching equation: salicylic acid + acetic anhydride → aspirin + ethanoic acid; all coefficients are 1. Molar masses are 138.12 g/mol, 102.09 g/mol and 180.16 g/mol for the two reactants and aspirin respectively. Use n=m/M, minimum reactant amount/coefficient, and yield%=100×isolated/theoretical mass. The isolate mass can include impurities or solvent; mass yield does not establish purity or conversion. Inputs are constructed teaching quantities, not a protocol.
