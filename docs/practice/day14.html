Day 14: Exercises — Reaction-mechanism review

Choose, draw and audit substitution and elimination mechanisms, then explain how competing pathways differ. Revisit the substitution and elimination and addition lectures, or inspect the SN1 demo.

Core 1–8: about 80–100 minutes. Transfer 9–12: an optional 30–45 minutes. Draw before opening a check; compare reasoning with a partner and keep your own corrected drawing.

Refresher and worked start

Full-headed arrows move pairs from bonds/lone pairs; ordinary carbon has total bond order four. Alpha is the leaving-group carbon; beta is its neighbour. E2 uses an appropriate anti geometry and three linked pair movements. Selected carbonyl reduction gives alkoxide then alcohol after proton transfer.

For the worked starting example OH⁻+CH₃Br→CH₃OH+Br⁻, draw OH pair→C and C–Br pair→Br. Both sides have net charge −1; the concerted step contains no carbocation. Use the same atom/charge audit in the problems below.

1. Find two reacting centres

Draw CH₃CH₂Br and CH₃CH(Br)CH₂CH₃. Mark the Br-bearing carbon and every beta carbon. Which substrate has the more accessible SN2 centre?

Hint

Compare local neighbours rather than whole-molecule carbon totals.

Worked answer and reasoning

Ethyl bromide has a primary reaction centre and one beta carbon;2-bromobutane has a secondary centre and two beta carbons. The primary centre is less sterically obstructed for backside attack, all else comparable.

2. Build a carbon–oxygen bond

Assume suitable dry SN2 conditions for CH₃O⁻+CH₃CH₂Br. Draw both arrows and products, including charge.

Hint

Oxygen donates the pair; Br takes the old C–Br pair.

Worked answer and reasoning

Products are CH₃OCH₂CH₃ and Br⁻. Oxygen becomes a neutral two-bond ether oxygen. Net charge is −1 before and after. The new bond is to the substrate carbon, not to bromine.

3. Keep the nitrogen proton count

Draw the first step of NH₃ attack on CH₃CH₂Br in the selected SN2 model. Then draw a possible equilibrium proton transfer to another NH₃ molecule.

Hint

Separate bond formation from a later proton transfer.

Worked answer and reasoning

First obtain CH₃CH₂NH₃⁺+Br⁻. Another ammonia takes H⁺ to give CH₃CH₂NH₂+NH₄⁺. Neutral ethylamine is not the immediate attack product, because nitrogen initially retains all three H atoms. No quantitative equilibrium extent is asserted by these formal steps; excess ammonia and the actual conditions matter.

4. Repair a charge-inconsistent mechanism

A drawing shows H₂O adding to (CH₃)₃C⁺ and immediately labels the product(CH₃)₃COH. Supply the missing intermediate and proton-transfer step.

Hint

Water oxygen has two H atoms when it first bonds to carbon.

Worked answer and reasoning

The immediate product is(CH₃)₃COH₂⁺. Water then removes H⁺, giving (CH₃)₃COH+H₃O⁺. This preserves the original H atoms and positive charge through the steps.

5. Draw one anti view

Look from a beta carbon toward the Br-bearing alpha carbon of an alkyl bromide. Put reacting beta-H at twelve o’clock and rear Br at six. Draw a Newman view and name the angle.

Hint

Front bonds start at the dot; rear bonds at the circle.

Worked answer and reasoning

The H–C–C–Br dihedral is 180°, anti-periplanar. Add the remaining substituents consistently to each carbon; the selected H and Br are on opposite sides of the viewed bond.

6. Turn that view into E2

Using that anti arrangement and B⁻, draw the E2 step and account for all three pairs.

Hint

Do not leave the C–H pair without a destination.

Worked answer and reasoning

B pair→H forms BH; C–H pair→C–C forms the pi bond; C–Br pair→Br gives Br⁻. The step is concerted and has no carbocation intermediate.

7. Choose possible alkene positions

Draw all constitutional alkenes from formal HBr loss from 2-bromobutane. State which has E/Z forms and one reason products need not be equally abundant.

Hint

Test each beta site and then apply the two-different-groups rule at each double-bond carbon.

Worked answer and reasoning

The constitutional products are but-1-ene and but-2-ene. But-2-ene has E/Z forms; but-1-ene does not. Geometry, base sterics and other conditions can make pathways unequally favoured.

8. Map the SN1/E1 branch

From(CH₃)₃C⁺ in water, draw carbon capture and beta-H loss as separate branches, keeping charges visible.

Hint

Water can donate a pair to carbon or to H.

Worked answer and reasoning

Carbon capture gives oxonium and then alcohol after deprotonation; beta-H loss gives2-methylpropene+H₃O⁺. These are the SN1 and E1 branches after ionisation. A common cation does not imply a unique final product.

Note

Optional transfer problems 9–12. Select for revision; they are not additional required contact hours.

9. Invert and reassign

Assume SN2 replacement of Br by OH in(R)-2-bromobutane. Draw the inverted geometry and independently assign the product descriptor.

Hint

For this case, Br/OH rank first, ethyl second, methyl third and H fourth.

Worked answer and reasoning

Inversion produces(S)-butan-2-ol for this particular priority ordering. The geometric inversion is the mechanism rule; the R/S change follows only after checking the actual replacement priorities.

10. Add HBr with a reason

For ionic HBr addition to propene without peroxides, draw protonation and capture. Explain the major connectivity using the two possible carbocations.

Hint

Compare secondary and primary cation formation.

Worked answer and reasoning

Protonation giving CH₃CH⁺CH₃ is favoured over CH₃CH₂CH₂⁺ in the selected model. Br⁻ capture gives2-bromopropane. Move the alkene pi pair to H and the H–Br pair to Br before drawing capture.

11. Distinguish a bridged intermediate

Sketch bromonium opening by Br⁻ and explain why the relative addition is anti.

Hint

Show opposite-side carbon attack plus return of the bridge-bond pair to Br.

Worked answer and reasoning

Backside attack opens the corresponding C–Br bridge bond, placing the new bromine opposite the bridging bromine. This is a different intermediate and stereochemical account from the free-carbocation HBr model.

12. Write a justified pathway comparison

Compare methyl bromide+OH⁻ with tertiary bromide in an ionising aqueous medium. Give a plausible pathway for each and identify one unsupported claim to avoid.

Hint

Use steric access and cation stability, then remember competing elimination.

Worked answer and reasoning

Methyl supports the concerted SN2 example and has no beta carbon for ordinary elimination. Tertiary bromide can ionise and undergo water capture or E1. Avoid promising an exact substitution/elimination ratio without actual conditions or data.

Explore the chemistry

Prediction, exploration and explanation

Predict (1 minute). Draw the first electron-pair operation for a tertiary bromide in the selected aqueous SN1 route. Which ion receives the old C–Br pair?

Explore (3–5 minutes). Choose an operation, try it and read the feedback. Work from tertiary bromide through carbocation and oxonium to neutral alcohol. Deliberately try the neutral-alcohol shortcut or deletion of an H. Find the precise atom/charge problem. Watch bromide and both explicit waters through the sequence.

Explain (2 minutes). Write the complete net equation including water, hydronium and bromide. Explain why the positive charge moves from carbon to oxygen before the final proton transfer, and why an explicit proton acceptor is essential.

Supplied model and its limits

The substrate is (CH₃)₃CBr, and the selected branch is ionisation→water capture→water deprotonation. Start with two waters: one becomes the alcohol oxygen and the other receives the extra proton. Br⁻ remains after ionisation. Total charge of all displayed species is zero. E1 can compete in real conditions, but its proportion is not calculated here. The choices test a stated mechanistic model, not a universal reaction selector.
