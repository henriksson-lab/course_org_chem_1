Day 4: Exercises — Structure and property review

Read each structure, explain its properties and check the acid–base predictions.

Core 1–8: about 75–95 minutes including comparison and correction. Extension 9–12: about 30–45 minutes for optional independent work. Try each problem before opening its hint, then use the worked answer to check your reasoning.

Refresher and worked starting point

Use these rules: ordinary neutral C has bond-order sum4; neutral O has two bonds/two lone pairs; skeletal vertices/endpoints are carbon. Donors here need O–H/N–H, while acceptors need suitable available pairs. For the supplied monoprotic acid model, r=10^(pH−pKa), fraction=r/(1+r). Worked start: CH₃CH₂OH is C₂H₆O, has an OH donor and O acceptor; at pH=pKa an acid has fraction0.5.

Local lessons: structures, properties, acids bases.

1. Recover two chains

Draw unbranched four-carbon and five-carbon saturated skeletal chains. Label all terminal H counts and give formulas.

Hint

Count vertices, not line segments.

Worked check

Butane has C₄H₁₀ and pentane C₅H₁₂. Terminal C carries3H; each internal chain C has 2H.

2. Show a genuine constitutional difference

Draw propan-1-ol and methoxyethane CH₃OCH₂CH₃. Verify their shared formula and explain an oxygen-neighbour difference.

Hint

One has an O–H bond; the other has two O–C bonds.

Worked check

Both are C₃H₈O. The alcohol O neighbours C/H; ether O neighbours C/C. Their connectivity differs.

3. Audit all oxygens in an acid

Draw CH₃COOH and CH₃COO⁻. Assign relevant O bonds, lone pairs and net charges using one valid contributor for the anion.

Hint

Negative O with one bond has three lone pairs.

Worked check

Neutral acid has carbonyl O with two lone pairs and OH O with two. One ethanoate contributor has singly bonded O⁻ with three pairs; the other O is double-bonded. Net charges0 and−1.

4. Predict a donor/acceptor contrast

Compare ethanol, dimethyl ether and propanone. Draw each and mark donor/acceptor sites.

Hint

A carbonyl oxygen can accept even without OH.

Worked check

Ethanol donates/accepts. Ether and propanone accept but lack O–H/N–H donor bonds. Do not invent H atoms on their oxygens.

5. Explain boiling rather than bond breaking

Use approximate normal boiling points ethanol78 ° C and dimethyl ether−24 ° C. Give a controlled interaction comparison and state what ordinary boiling preserves.

Hint

They share a molecular formula and similar size.

Worked check

Ethanol can form an OH hydrogen-bond network; ether lacks an OH donor. Ordinary boiling separates largely intact molecules, preserving C–O connectivity.

6. Test two spectral candidates

A suitable complete IR has strong C=O near 1715 cm⁻¹ and no alcohol OH feature. Candidates are propanone and propan-1-ol. Give predictions and conclusion.

Hint

Compare expected bonds before naming the unknown.

Worked check

Propanone predicts C=O and no OH; alcohol predicts OH and no C=O. The observations support propanone within this pair, not unique identity among all compounds.

7. Draw a proton-transfer pair

Draw CH₃COOH+CH₃NH₂⇌CH₃COO⁻+CH₃NH₃⁺ using two arrows. Check net charge.

Hint

Start at N lone pair and acid O–H bond.

Worked check

N pair→H; O–H pair→O. Reactant charge0 equals product−1+1=0. Both conjugate pairs differ by H⁺.

8. Calculate a midpoint and a nearby fraction

Use pKa 4.8. Obtain A⁻/HA and ionised fraction at pH 4.8 and 5.8.

Hint

Convert ratio to fraction with its full denominator.

Worked check

Ratios1 and 10 give fractions0.5 and 10/11≈0.9091. A ratio of 10 is not a fraction of 10.

Optional extension

Use these after the core or during later revision.

9. Repair a misleading functional-group label

A student calls CH₃COOCH₃ an ether because it contains C–O–C. Explain what they omitted.

Hint

Draw the adjacent C=O explicitly.

Worked check

The single-bonded O attaches to a carbonyl carbon, forming an ester motif. Local connectivity includes the carbonyl; it is not an ordinary simple ether.

10. Check a substitution effect on acidity

Supplied comparable aqueous pKa values:ethanoic acid4.8, chloroethanoic acid2.9. Explain which acid is stronger using conjugate-base stabilisation.

Hint

Cl withdraws electron density through sigma bonds.

Worked check

Chloroethanoic acid is stronger. Inductive withdrawal stabilises its conjugate base. The inference concerns compatible equilibrium data, not reaction rate.

11. Distinguish three charges

Explain formal charge on hydroxide, partial negative charge on ethanol O, and net charge of water.

Hint

These labels describe different levels of bookkeeping.

Worked check

Hydroxide O has formal−1; ethanol O is formally0 but δ− in polar bonds; water has net0. Net neutrality does not prevent polar bonds or a molecular dipole.

12. Use a measured mass without a spreadsheet

Using supplied molar mass138.12 g/mol, calculate amount for 0.27624 g. Give mol/mmol and show unit cancellation.

Hint

n=m/M.

Worked check

0.002000 mol=2.000 mmol. Dividing g by g/mol cancels grams and leaves moles. This is not a concentration without a solution volume.

Review your solutions

Choose one corrected drawing and one corrected explanation. State the observation or rule that made you change your answer. Save these beside your course notes; extra R tasks belong to their separate unit practice pages.

Take-away

Choose two molecules with oxygen and explain why one local O–H or C–O bond does not classify all their functional groups.

Model and limits

The workbench recognises the complete motifs supplied in these particular structures; it is not an automatic general chemical classifier. Highlighted atoms identify the chosen local site. Neutral drawn glycine is used to inspect connectivity; actual aqueous protonation depends on pH.
