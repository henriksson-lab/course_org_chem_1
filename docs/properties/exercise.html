Structure, interactions and IR: standalone practice bank

Use the lecture and the refresher below; no textbook or external problem sheet is needed. Core 1–12: about 60–80 minutes with drawings and checks. Transfer 13–20: an optional 35–50 minutes for consolidation or later revision. Choose a smaller core subset when a practical session also includes R. The separate computing task does not count as paper chemistry.

Tools and worked start

Bond partial charge describes unequal sharing; formal charge is a Lewis integer count. Hydrogen-bond donors here have suitable O–H/N–H bonds; acceptors have suitable available lone pairs. In IR, absorption is a dip on a transmittance plot and an upward feature on absorbance. Supplied approximate teaching ranges: alcohol OH3200–3600 cm⁻¹; acid OH often 2500–3300 cm⁻¹; carbonyl often 1650–1800 cm⁻¹. These ranges are not unique identities. Worked start: ether O can accept from water, but no ether O–H exists to donate.

Draw first, explain the rule you used, and open the check only after an attempt. Ordinary drawings may use a structure editor or paper.

1. Assign two kinds of charge

Draw ethanol with O lone pairs. Mark C–O partial charges and calculate O formal charge. Explain why the two labels differ.

Hint

Unequal sharing differs from a Lewis integer count.

Worked check

C is δ+ and O δ− in the C–O bond. Ethanol O has formal charge zero with two bonds/two pairs. A partial charge is not a whole added electron.

2. Combine dipoles geometrically

Sketch linear O=C=O and bent H₂O. Indicate bond-dipole directions toward O and predict each molecular sum.

Hint

Equal opposite vectors cancel; angled ones need not.

Worked check

CO₂ dipoles cancel by symmetry. Bent water has a nonzero net dipole. Polar bonds alone do not establish a polar molecule.

3. Draw a donor–acceptor contact

Draw ethanol and water and mark one O–H···O hydrogen bond. Distinguish the covalent and intermolecular connections.

Hint

The donor supplies the H; the acceptor supplies a lone pair.

Worked check

Either molecule can donate OH to the other O. Covalent O–H stays within a molecule, while the H···O contact is dashed and does not change connectivity.

4. Let ether accept

Draw CH₃OCH₃ with water as a hydrogen-bond partner. Which molecule supplies H? Can pure ether provide an O–H donor?

Hint

Ether O has two C neighbours and no attached H.

Worked check

Water donates an O–H hydrogen to ether O. Ether can accept but has no O–H donor; oxygen presence alone does not imply donor ability.

5. Compare amines by their actual bonds

For CH₃NH₂, (CH₃)₂NH and (CH₃)₃N, identify N–H donor bonds and the neutral N acceptor pair.

Hint

Count N–H before counting carbon substituents.

Worked check

All three neutral amines have a lone-pair acceptor. The first two have N–H donor bonds; trimethylamine lacks N–H and cannot donate at N.

6. Test a protonated amine

Draw CH₃NH₃⁺ and compare its N donor/acceptor inventory with CH₃NH₂.

Hint

The original lone pair has become a bond.

Worked check

Methylammonium has N–H donors but no ordinary N lone-pair acceptor. It is not a neutral amine with the same available pair.

7. Explain a controlled boiling comparison

Ethanol and dimethyl ether share C₂H₆O. Their approximate normal boiling temperatures are 78 ° C and−24 ° C. Explain a structural reason for the difference.

Hint

Keep molecular size and pressure comparable.

Worked check

Ethanol forms an OH donor/acceptor network; the ether lacks an OH donor. This supports stronger liquid intermolecular attractions in ethanol, not stronger covalent C–O bonds.

8. Separate boiling from decomposition

A student says boiling ethanol breaks C–O bonds. Rewrite their claim to identify which interactions are normally overcome.

Hint

Ask whether vapour molecules still have the same connectivity.

Worked check

Boiling separates largely intact ethanol molecules by overcoming intermolecular attractions. C–O cleavage would be a chemical transformation, not ordinary vapour formation.

9. Compare related alcohols in water

Draw methanol and hexan-1-ol CH₃(CH₂)₅OH. Explain which is more water compatible and what a single-OH rule misses.

Hint

Balance the polar site against nonpolar size.

Worked check

Methanol is more water compatible. The longer alcohol has a much larger hydrocarbon region per OH group. The prediction is qualitative and depends on conditions.

10. Read an IR transmission feature

A complete IR transmission trace dips strongly near 1715 cm⁻¹. Name the axes and explain what the dip means.

Hint

Less transmission corresponds to more absorption.

Worked check

The horizontal coordinate is wavenumber; the vertical coordinate is transmittance. The dip supports absorption near 1715 cm⁻¹, often a carbonyl feature, without uniquely identifying a molecule.

11. Change the plotting convention

A second graph shows an upward band at the same wavenumber but labels its y-axis absorbance. Is it contradictory evidence?

Hint

The ordinate changes the feature direction.

Worked check

No. An absorbance band points upward while transmission decreases for the same absorption. Interpret axes before calling a plot wrong.

12. Choose among three candidates

Unknown has strong C=O near 1715 cm⁻¹ and no broad alcohol OH region in suitable data. Candidates: ethanol, propanone, dimethyl ether. Choose and justify.

Hint

Draw the functional groups predicted by each.

Worked check

Propanone best fits this list: it has C=O and lacks OH. The other candidates lack carbonyl. This restricted comparison does not establish absolute identity or purity.

Optional transfer and challenge

These extend the same concepts without assuming later reaction chemistry.

13. Reject a unique-carbonyl claim

A student identifies propanone from one C=O band. Draw another three-carbon carbonyl compound and explain the limitation.

Hint

An aldehyde can contain the same total carbon count.

Worked check

Propanal CH₃CH₂CHO also contains C=O. One broad-region observation is shared by many structures; further spectral and other evidence is required.

14. Inspect sample contamination

An intended ether sample has a broad OH feature. Give two explanations consistent with limited evidence without choosing one as certain.

Hint

A feature can belong to the target or another component.

Worked check

The sample could contain an OH-bearing impurity or water; the target assignment could also be wrong. More observations are needed to distinguish these explanations.

15. Distinguish alcohol and acid OH

Draw ethanol and ethanoic acid. Which has a neighbouring carbonyl, and how can the expected broad IR regions differ?

Hint

Acid OH is part of C(=O)OH.

Worked check

Ethanoic acid has OH adjacent to carbonyl and often an especially broad acid-OH region around 2500–3300 cm⁻¹. Alcohol OH is commonly broad around 3200–3600 cm⁻¹; exact shapes depend on conditions.

16. Check an ester inventory

For CH₃COOCH₃, identify the carbonyl and single-bonded O. Does the molecule have any O–H donor?

Hint

Write each oxygen bond explicitly.

Worked check

It has two oxygen environments able to accept hydrogen-bond contacts in this simple treatment, but no OH donor. It is an ester, not an alcohol plus an unrelated ether.

17. Interpret ion hydration

Compare CH₃COOH and CH₃COO⁻ in water. Name an interaction made especially important by the latter charge.

Hint

Water is polar even though neutral overall.

Worked check

Carboxylate can engage in ion–dipole hydration. The neutral acid also interacts with water, but formal charge changes the interaction pattern. Exact solubility still needs conditions and additional thermodynamics.

18. Repair an IR-to-solubility shortcut

Rewrite: “It absorbs IR, so it must dissolve in water.” Give separate valid claims about IR and solubility.

Hint

A changing vibrational dipole is not an entire solution free energy.

Worked check

IR absorption probes suitable vibrational transitions. Water solubility depends on the balance of interactions, size, charge, condensed-phase structure and conditions. A band alone does not establish it.

19. Use two observations together

Unknown is one of propan-1-ol or propanal. Suitable IR has C=O and no broad alcohol OH. Give one prediction for each candidate and a restrained conclusion.

Hint

Test both presence and absence.

Worked check

The alcohol predicts OH and lacks C=O; the aldehyde predicts C=O and lacks alcohol OH. Propanal fits the two observations among this pair; further evidence would address identity beyond this list.

20. Name the missing extraction inputs

A monoprotic acid becomes mostly ionised in water at high pH. Why can that fact alone not predict mass recovered into an organic solvent?

Hint

Two phases require a partition model and their quantities.

Worked check

Aqueous speciation supplies only species ratios. Partition coefficients, phase volumes, starting amount and losses affect recovered mass. More ionisation is not a complete recovery calculation.

Review your work

Choose one corrected mistake and explain the chemical rule that repairs it. Continue with the self-check. The reading guide offers optional background, not required problem inputs.
