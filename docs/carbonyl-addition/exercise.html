Day 16: Exercises — Carbonyl addition

Draw carbonyl-addition mechanisms, track oxygen atoms and check each proton transfer. Use problems 1–12 for core work (about 80–100 minutes); choose from 13–20 for optional transfer work (another 45–65 minutes), focusing on the steps you need to practise.

Local refresher and worked starting example

The polar C=O bond makes carbon electrophilic. Addition forms a new bond to carbon while the pi pair goes to oxygen, yielding a tetrahedral species. Proton transfers use actual donors/bases. Hydrate=two OH; hemiacetal=OH+OR; acetal=OR+OR at one carbon. Full-headed arrows move pairs, and R/S requires a defined 3D view.

Worked starting example. OH⁻ adds to CH₃CHO with OH pair→C and C=O pair→O, giving CH₃CH(OH)O⁻. Water then donates H to that alkoxide and becomes OH⁻. The hydrate is neutral CH₃CH(OH)₂; net reaction is aldehyde+water⇌hydrate, not spontaneous loss of oxygen or creation of a proton.

Draw structures, identify the relevant atom and explain the reason. A name alone is insufficient where a mechanism or argument is requested.

1. Separate partial and formal charge

Draw neutral CH₃CHO with oxygen lone pairs, δ labels and formal charges.

Hint

Unequal electron sharing need not create integer Lewis charges.

Worked answer and reasoning

O is δ− and carbonyl C δ+, while their ordinary neutral Lewis formal charges are both zero. Partial charge explains polarity; it is not a pre-existing carbocation formula.

2. Count carbonyl geometry

Draw CH₃COCH₃ and count its carbonyl-carbon neighbours and bond orders. State the approximate geometry before addition.

Hint

There are three bonded atoms but one double bond.

Worked answer and reasoning

Carbonyl C has three atom neighbours and total bond order four, approximately trigonal planar. Confusing neighbour count with bond-order sum can produce an incorrect valence audit.

3. Draw hydroxide addition

For CH₃CHO+OH⁻, draw two coupled arrows and the tetrahedral intermediate.

Hint

The carbonyl pi pair must move when the new bond forms.

Worked answer and reasoning

OH lone pair→C and C=O pair→O give CH₃CH(OH)O⁻. Carbon has four single bonds, and the original carbonyl oxygen bears the negative charge.

4. Track oxygen labels

Label carbonyl O as A and incoming hydroxide O as B in the preceding step. Which becomes O⁻ and which is OH?

Hint

The incoming hydroxide keeps its H during C–O bond formation.

Worked answer and reasoning

A becomes O⁻ after receiving the π pair. B is the newly attached OH. Oxygen identities must not be exchanged without extra chemical steps.

5. Complete hydration

Use water to protonate the preceding alkoxide, drawing all products.

Hint

Water becomes hydroxide when it loses H⁺.

Worked answer and reasoning

The alkoxide pair takes H from water while the water O–H pair returns to O. Products are CH₃CH(OH)₂ and OH⁻. Hydroxide used in the first step is regenerated overall.

6. Write the net hydrated equation

Combine hydroxide addition and proton transfer, then cancel the regenerated catalyst.

Hint

OH⁻ occurs on both sides of the two-step sum.

Worked answer and reasoning

The net equation is CH₃CHO+H₂O⇌CH₃CH(OH)₂. The alkoxide is an intermediate and hydroxide a catalyst in this model. This does not specify which side dominates equilibrium.

7. Activate with acid

Draw protonation of CH₃CHO oxygen by H₃O⁺ with both arrows and the charge on the product.

Hint

The carbonyl oxygen lone pair accepts a proton; hydronium loses it as water forms.

Worked answer and reasoning

Products are CH₃CH=OH⁺ and H₂O. The protonated oxygen has three bond orders and positive formal charge; carbon becomes more electrophilic. Do not assign an unexplained extra H to carbon.

8. Choose a compatible nucleophile

For excess acidic methanol, should the selected elementary attack use neutral CH₃OH or freely persistent CH₃O⁻? Explain.

Hint

Check the strong base against the proton-rich medium.

Worked answer and reasoning

Neutral methanol is the suitable nucleophile for the selected acid-catalysed route. Methoxide is strongly basic and would be protonated in such a medium, so assuming it freely persists ignores acid–base chemistry.

9. Attack the activated carbonyl

Draw methanol addition to CH₃CH=OH⁺, including movement of the carbonyl pi pair. Count incoming oxygen bonds afterward.

Hint

The formerly carbonyl oxygen becomes neutral OH; incoming methanol oxygen gains a bond.

Worked answer and reasoning

The intermediate is CH₃CH(OH)(O⁺HCH₃). Incoming oxygen has three single bonds and charge +1; original oxygen now has two single bonds and is neutral. Net charge +1 is retained.

10. Finish the hemiacetal

Use water to deprotonate CH₃CH(OH)(O⁺HCH₃). Identify the group that loses H and all products.

Hint

Neutralise the oxygen that has three bonds.

Worked answer and reasoning

Water accepts H from the incoming methanol-derived oxonium group, giving CH₃CH(OH)OCH₃ and H₃O⁺. The product is a neutral hemiacetal, and acid is regenerated.

11. Classify four products

Classify CH₃CHO, CH₃CH(OH)₂, CH₃CH(OH)OCH₃ and CH₃CH(OCH₃)₂ by local functional pattern.

Hint

Count OH and OR groups attached to the same carbon.

Worked answer and reasoning

They are aldehyde, hydrate, hemiacetal and acetal respectively. One OH plus one OR defines the hemiacetal; two OR groups define the acetal. An ether elsewhere in a molecule is not enough.

12. Reject an unexplained acetal jump

A proposed arrow converts a hemiacetal OH directly into OCH₃ without another species or intermediate. Explain why it is incomplete.

Hint

A poor leaving group and a new incoming alcohol both need accounting.

Worked answer and reasoning

Acetal formation requires additional acid-mediated activation, loss of water, alcohol addition and proton transfers. Merely swapping labels omits bond changes, atom sources and charge bookkeeping.

Note

Optional transfer starts here. Choose problems that address your own gaps; the core route remains1–12.

13. Balance acetal formation

Balance CH₃CHO+CH₃OH⇌CH₃CH(OCH₃)₂+H₂O by finding the required methanol coefficient.

Hint

The acetal contains two methoxy groups.

Worked answer and reasoning

Two methanol molecules are required: CH₃CHO+2CH₃OH⇌CH₃CH(OCH₃)₂+H₂O. Carbon, oxygen and hydrogen then balance. Acid catalyses the path and is not consumed in the net equation.

14. Change water composition

For that acid-catalysed equilibrium, predict the direction favoured by excess water and explain the acid role separately.

Hint

Water is on the product side of acetal formation.

Worked answer and reasoning

Excess water can favour hydrolysis toward carbonyl plus methanol. Acid accelerates interconversion in both directions without changing K for the same net reaction and conditions. Changing composition and adding catalyst are different operations.

15. Count a cyclic hemiacetal ring

Draw HOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂CHO and link its terminal alcohol O to aldehyde carbon through the hemiacetal pattern. Count ring atoms.

Hint

The attacking oxygen becomes part of the ring.

Worked answer and reasoning

The ring contains one oxygen and five carbons, six atoms total. The original carbonyl oxygen becomes OH at the former aldehyde carbon. Leaving the terminal oxygen outside the ring would lose the actual new C–O bond.

16. Audit cyclisation formula

Compare the molecular formula before and after neutral cyclic hemiacetal formation from 5-hydroxypentanal. Is water lost overall?

Hint

Addition and proton transfer do not require removal of a whole molecule.

Worked answer and reasoning

The formula remains C₅H₁₀O₂. No net water is lost in hemiacetal cyclisation. Confusing this with later acetal formation would incorrectly subtract H₂O.

17. Compare carbonyl substituents

Draw CH₃CHO and CH₃C(=O)OCH₃. Which has an oxygen-based substituent at carbonyl C that can participate in a suitable addition–elimination route?

Hint

Inspect the group bonded directly to the carbonyl carbon.

Worked answer and reasoning

The ester has OCH₃; the aldehyde has H. Appropriate acyl substitution can involve departure of an oxygen-based group, whereas treating H⁻ as a normal leaving group is not a sound explanation for ordinary aqueous aldehyde addition.

18. Ask whether a new stereocentre forms

Methanol forms a hemiacetal from ethanal. List the four substituents at the former carbonyl carbon and test for a stereocentre.

Hint

The carbonyl carbon becomes tetrahedral.

Worked answer and reasoning

The groups are CH₃, H, OH and OCH₃, four different groups, so a stereocentre forms. Both faces of a planar aldehyde may react in an achiral environment; an R/S result needs an actual 3D product drawing.

19. Contrast a symmetric ketone

Methanol adds to propanone to give(CH₃)₂C(OH)OCH₃. Does the former carbonyl carbon become a stereocentre?

Hint

Compare the identities of its two carbon groups.

Worked answer and reasoning

No. Its groups include two identical CH₃ groups, in addition to OH and OCH₃. Becoming tetrahedral is not alone sufficient for a simple stereocentre.

20. Separate pathway from population

A student draws a valid hydrate mechanism and concludes the sample must be100% hydrate. Explain what evidence is missing.

Hint

A route describes how species interconvert; an equilibrium describes their relative amounts.

Worked answer and reasoning

A plausible mechanism does not supply the equilibrium constant or species concentrations. Structure, solvent, temperature and equilibrium evidence determine the mixture. The hydrate can be accessible without being the dominant form.

Explore

Track oxygen labels and formal charges through hydrate and hemiacetal formation. Allow 7–10 minutes: predict first, change one input at a time and explain the result.

Explore the chemistry

Prediction, exploration and explanation

Predict (1 minute). Label aldehyde oxygen A and incoming nucleophile oxygen B. During initial hydroxide addition, which oxygen becomes negative? During methanol attack on a protonated aldehyde, which oxygen temporarily becomes positive?

Explore (4–5 minutes). Compare hydrate and hemiacetal routes. Step forward and backward, select each oxygen, and assign its formal charge plus the proton-transfer role entering that state. Use the check feedback. Pay special attention to oxygen C in water/hydronium and to the fact that addition itself is not a proton-transfer step.

Explain (2 minutes). Draw one labelled complete sequence. In base-catalysed hydration, why can the regenerated OH⁻ have oxygen C even though the incoming OH⁻ had oxygen B? In the acid route, which water accepts the final proton, and where is the catalyst at the end?

Supplied model and its limits

A always denotes the original carbonyl oxygen; B the incoming hydroxide or methanol oxygen; C the hydronium/water proton-transfer partner. The hydrate route has net charge −1 across all explicit species; the acid-catalysed hemiacetal route has net charge +1. Atom labels track identities, while catalyst regeneration means regeneration of a species, not necessarily the same individually labelled oxygen. These simplified paths do not predict equilibrium composition or supply operating instructions.

Carry the explanation into practice

Return to the local exercises. Keep your prediction, a corrected drawing or calculation, and two sentences stating what the model supports and what it leaves unresolved.
