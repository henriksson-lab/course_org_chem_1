Day 1: Exercises — Reading organic structures

Draw each structure and check its atoms, bonds and charges. Use the lecture and the refresher below when needed.

Core 1–12: about 60–80 minutes with drawings and checks. Transfer 13–20: an optional 35–50 minutes for consolidation or later revision. Choose a smaller core subset when the session also includes R.

Tools and worked start

For ordinary neutral structures use C bond-order sum 4, N three bonds/one lone pair, O two bonds/two lone pairs. Skeletal endpoints/corners are carbon; carbon-bound H is usually implicit. A double/triple bond counts 2/3. Formal charge equals valence electrons minus nonbonding electrons minus half the bonding electrons. Use valence-electron counts C4, N 5, O 6. Worked start: CH₃CH₂OH has two C, six H, one O and formula C₂H₆O.

Draw first, explain the rule you used, and open the check only after an attempt. Ordinary drawings may use a structure editor or paper.

1. Recover a five-carbon line

Draw an unbranched four-segment skeletal zigzag with only single bonds. Label each carbon CH₃ or CH₂ and obtain its molecular formula.

Hint

Segments are bonds; corners and endpoints are atoms.

Worked check

Five carbons give CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃. The H count is 3+2+2+2+3=12, so the formula is C₅H₁₂.

2. Read a branch

Expand (CH₃)₃CH. Draw its three C–C bonds, count atoms and explain whether it has the same connectivity as CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃.

Hint

The subscript three repeats the complete parenthesised methyl group.

Worked check

There are four C and ten H. A central CH connects to three methyl carbons. This is 2-methylpropane, a constitutional isomer of unbranched butane, not simply another view.

3. Introduce a double bond

Draw CH₃CH=CHCH₂CH₃ with explicit H at both alkene carbons. Compare its formula with the saturated five-carbon chain.

Hint

Each end of C=C uses two bond orders in that connection.

Worked check

The structure is C₅H₁₀. Each alkene carbon has one H; the extra bond order replaces one H at each end relative to pentane.

4. Read a terminal triple bond

Expand CH₃C≡CH and give the H count at all three carbons. Is its middle carbon CH or C?

Hint

Single plus triple already totals four bond orders.

Worked check

The counts are 3,0,1 and the formula is C₃H₄. The middle carbon is C with no H. The terminal alkyne carbon bears one H.

5. Distinguish oxygen neighbours

Draw ethanol CH₃CH₂OH and dimethyl ether CH₃OCH₃ with oxygen lone pairs. State formula and O neighbours for each.

Hint

Count H after drawing the connectivity.

Worked check

Both are C₂H₆O. Ethanol O connects to C and H; ether O to two C atoms. Each neutral O has two bonds/two lone pairs, but the molecules are constitutional isomers.

6. Audit an oxygen series

Draw H₂O, OH⁻ and H₃O⁺ with all O lone pairs. Calculate charge using FC=6−nonbonding electrons−half the bonding electrons.

Hint

A lone pair contains two electrons.

Worked check

Water has two bonds/two pairs/FC0; hydroxide one bond/three pairs/FC−1; hydronium three bonds/one pair/FC+1. Show each charge explicitly.

7. Audit nitrogen protonation

Draw CH₃NH₂ and CH₃NH₃⁺ with relevant nitrogen lone pairs. Use five valence electrons for N to explain the charge change.

Hint

Compare three bonds/one pair with four bonds/no pair.

Worked check

Methylamine N has FC5−2−3=0. Methylammonium N has FC5−0−4=+1. The original pair forms the new N–H bond.

8. Read carbonyl parentheses

Expand CH₃C(=O)OH. Count atoms and carbonyl-carbon neighbours; distinguish neighbour count from bond-order sum.

Hint

The double bond connects the carbon to one O atom twice.

Worked check

The formula is C₂H₄O₂. Carbonyl C has three atom neighbours: methyl C, carbonyl O and hydroxyl O. The bond-order sum is 1+2+1=4.

9. Classify five local patterns

Draw CH₃CH₂OH, CH₃OCH₃, CH₃CHO, CH₃COCH₃ and CH₃COOCH₃. Identify each functional group and circle all carbonyl carbons.

Hint

Rewrite CO as C(=O) where the indicated condensed carbonyl notation requires it.

Worked check

The groups are alcohol, ether, aldehyde, ketone and ester. The last three contain carbonyls; only the alcohol in this list has an O–H bond.

10. Name two alcohol positions

Draw CH₃CH₂CH₂OH and CH₃CH(OH)CH₃. Name each by a three-carbon parent chain and give the OH locant.

Hint

Number to identify the OH-bearing carbon consistently.

Worked check

They are propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol. They are constitutional isomers: in the second, OH is attached to the central rather than terminal carbon.

11. Repair a carbon valence error

A neutral proposed carbon has C=O, C–C and three C–H bonds. Count its bond orders and repair it to an aldehyde carbon.

Hint

Ordinary second-row carbon cannot keep six bond orders.

Worked check

The proposed sum is 2+1+3=6. In CH₃C(=O)H, the carbonyl carbon instead has C=O, C–C and one C–H, summing to four.

12. Connect geometry with bonding

For CH₄, H₂C=CH₂ and HC≡CH, state local carbon geometry and approximate bond angles. Which drawing needs a depth convention?

Hint

Use tetrahedral, trigonal planar and linear.

Worked check

Methane is tetrahedral (~109.5°), ethene carbons trigonal planar (~120°), and ethyne carbons linear(180°). A full methane 3D drawing needs wedge/dash or another depth convention.

13. Do not create an isomer by reversing text

Compare CH₃CH₂CH₂OH with HOCH₂CH₂CH₃. List neighbours of O and the OH-bearing carbon. Are they distinct?

Hint

Trace connectivity rather than left/right order.

Worked check

They are the same propan-1-ol. O attaches to H and terminal CH₂; that carbon attaches to O, the next C and two H in either notation.

14. Find two groups in glycine

Expand neutral H₂NCH₂COOH, draw lone pairs and give the molecular formula. Do not assume any biological pathway knowledge.

Hint

Count N–H, C–H and O–H separately.

Worked check

Glycine contains an amine and carboxylic acid. Its neutral drawn form is C₂H₅NO₂:2 N–H+2 C–H+1 O–H. Other protonation forms will be considered later.

15. Read lactic acid completely

Expand CH₃CH(OH)COOH. Count atoms, classify both oxygen-containing groups and identify the carbon with four different substituents.

Hint

The parenthesised OH is a branch on CH.

Worked check

The formula is C₃H₆O₃, with alcohol and carboxylic acid groups. The middle carbon connects to OH, H, CH₃, COOH: four different groups.

16. Distinguish a diol from an acid

Compare HOCH₂CH₂OH and CH₃COOH. Both contain two O atoms. Explain why counting O alone cannot assign the group.

Hint

Draw all C–O bond orders.

Worked check

The first is a diol with two single-bonded OH groups and no carbonyl. The second is a carboxylic acid with one C=O and one O–H. Connectivity and bond orders distinguish them.

17. Find all H atoms in an ester

Draw CH₃C(=O)OCH₂CH₃. Give its molecular formula and the number of H on the carbonyl carbon.

Hint

The carbonyl C already has two single bonds plus its double bond.

Worked check

Ethyl ethanoate is C₄H₈O₂. Its H counts are 3,0,2,3 on the four carbons. The carbonyl carbon has no H.

18. Compare two skeletal naming choices

For CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₃, explain why numbering OH at 3 is unnecessary and give the preferred simple alcohol name.

Hint

Number the same chain from the opposite end.

Worked check

Butan-2-ol gives the OH the lower locant. Butan-3-ol describes the same connectivity with an inappropriate numbering direction, not a different isomer.

19. Calculate an amount manually

A teaching sample has mass 0.6906 g and supplied molar mass 138.12 g/mol. Use n=m/M and report mol and mmol.

Hint

Divide grams by grams per mole.

Worked check

n=0.005000 mol=5.000 mmol. The gram units cancel. This amount is half that represented by 1.3812 g at the same molar mass.

20. Explain a formula-only limitation

Give two distinct connected structures consistent with C₃H₈O, name their groups and state one additional kind of evidence that could distinguish them.

Hint

Consider moving OH or replacing an alcohol by an ether.

Worked check

Examples include propan-1-ol and methoxyethane CH₃OCH₂CH₃, an alcohol and ether. Structural or suitable spectral evidence is needed; a molecular formula alone does not decide.

R task: what amount did the balance give us?

Chemical question: how many moles are represented by a measured mass? Use the supplied relationship amount_mol = mass_g / molar_mass_g_mol. The molar mass has units g/mol; the division leaves mol. Multiply mol by 1000 to obtain mmol. Molar masses are supplied, not memorised.

Download and extract the student project. Open its RStudio project, then open report.Rmd. Keep input CSV files in data/. The table data/amounts.csv is a simulated teaching amount table; it is not a reaction protocol. First work the salicylic-acid row by hand: 1.3812 g at 138.12 g/mol should give 0.01000 mol.

library(readr)
library(dplyr)
amounts <- read_csv("data/amounts.csv", show_col_types = FALSE) |>
  mutate(amount_mol = mass_g / molar_mass_g_mol,
         amount_mmol = amount_mol * 1000)
knitr::kable(amounts, digits = 4)

Predict whether doubling a mass at fixed molar mass doubles the amount. Change an input in a copied R object, not in the raw CSV, and check. Add a sentence explaining what mmol measures. Knit the report from a fresh R session; an unrun object in the console should not be required.

Numerical and chemical check

Salicylic acid gives 10.00 mmol. With the supplied second row, 1.53135 g of acetic anhydride at 102.09 g/mol gives 15.00 mmol. Doubling either mass doubles its amount. These amounts alone do not select the limiting reagent: that requires the balanced reaction’s stoichiometric coefficients, introduced later. The unit mmol counts chemical amount, not volume, concentration or number of independent experiments.

Model and limits

These are neutral, ordinary-valence C/H/O examples. Carbon uses four bond orders and oxygen two. O-bound H is drawn explicitly; the reveal concerns implicit carbon-bound H. No charges, radicals or unusual valences are inferred.
