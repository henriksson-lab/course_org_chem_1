Day 12: Exercises — Elimination and addition

Locate the reacting atoms, draw elimination and addition steps, and use geometry to explain the products. Use the lecture and demo to revisit a worked case.

Core 1–12: about 80–100 minutes. Transfer 13–20: an optional 45–65 minutes; choose problems that target the mistakes you want to resolve.

Local refresher and worked starting example

Alpha means the carbon carrying the leaving group and beta an adjacent carbon. E2 couples base attack at β-H, C–H pair donation to C–C and leaving-group departure; a suitable anti-periplanar arrangement has180° H–C–C–leaving-group dihedral. Addition consumes an alkene pi bond. The ionic HBr cases here explicitly exclude peroxides.

Worked starting example. In a formal E2 step RCH₂CH₂Br+B⁻→RCH=CH₂+BH+Br⁻, three pairs move together: B→H, C–H→C–C and C–Br→Br. This balances atoms and charge, but a particular primary substrate may favour competing substitution under actual conditions.

Draw structures, identify the relevant atom and explain the reason. A name alone is insufficient where a mechanism or argument is requested.

1. Find every beta carbon

Draw 2-bromobutane and mark α and all β carbons. Explain the local definition.

Hint

Alpha is the carbon carrying the leaving group.

Worked answer and reasoning

C2 is α; C1 and C3 are β because they are directly adjacent to C2. Alpha/beta labels are relative to this functional site, not permanent names for the molecule.

2. List possible alkene connectivities

From formal HBr elimination of 2-bromobutane, draw but-1-ene and but-2-ene and show which β site produces each.

Hint

Remove H from either neighbouring carbon, without moving the carbon skeleton.

Worked answer and reasoning

Loss from C1 forms C1=C2, but-1-ene. Loss from C3 forms C2=C3, but-2-ene. This inventories possible connectivity, not product proportions in unspecified conditions.

3. Test the E/Z prerequisite

Draw the preceding two alkene products and decide which can be E/Z.

Hint

Each end of a double bond needs two different substituents.

Worked answer and reasoning

But-2-ene can be E or Z because each alkene carbon bears CH₃ and H. But-1-ene has two H on one end, so it cannot have an E/Z descriptor.

4. Construct an E2 electron-flow step

Assume a suitable anti conformation of 2-bromopropane and a strong base B⁻. Draw the three arrows and complete the products.

Hint

Base→β-H; C–H→C–C; C–Br→Br.

Worked answer and reasoning

Products are propene, BH and Br⁻. All three pair movements are concerted in E2; no carbocation is inserted. Net charge remains −1. The conformation must support the reacting geometry.

5. Reject methyl elimination

Explain why CH₃Br cannot undergo ordinary β-elimination to an alkene. Contrast it with CH₃CH₂Br as a formal bond inventory.

Hint

Count carbon atoms before searching for a β-H.

Worked answer and reasoning

Methyl bromide has no β-carbon. Ethyl bromide has one and can formally lose HBr to form ethene; however, a primary substrate with a strong nucleophile can favour SN2, so the inventory does not establish a dominant pathway.

6. Read a Newman projection

Looking from the β-carbon toward the α-carbon, put the front reacting H at twelve o’clock and rear Br at six. Give the H–C–C–Br dihedral.

Hint

Opposing clock positions are separated by half a turn.

Worked answer and reasoning

The dihedral is 180°, an anti-periplanar arrangement. The front bond starts at the dot and the rear bond at the circle. Reversing which carbon is viewed first requires redrawing the projection consistently.

7. Rotate without changing connectivity

Starting from that anti view, rotate only the rear carbon by 60°. Is the same H/Br pair still anti? Has the molecule become a constitutional isomer?

Hint

A torsion changes spatial arrangement around one bond.

Worked answer and reasoning

The selected pair is no longer at 180°. Connectivity and substituent identities remain unchanged, so this is a conformational change, not a constitutional isomerisation.

8. Fix a two-arrow E2 proposal

A drawing moves the base pair to β-H and the C–Br pair to Br, but never moves C–H electrons. What is missing?

Hint

Account for the pair left behind when H departs as H⁺.

Worked answer and reasoning

The C–H pair must form the new C–C π bond. Otherwise the proposed bond inventory and electron count do not describe the alkene. Draw all three arrows in the same elementary step.

9. Separate accessible from favoured products

A student lists all β sites, then claims each gives equal alkene amounts. Give two reasons that conclusion is unsupported.

Hint

Consider geometry and the identities of base and substrate.

Worked answer and reasoning

Different sites can have different access to anti geometry and give differently substituted alkenes; steric properties of the base and other conditions also affect relative pathways. Possible products need not be equally probable.

10. Draw E1 after ionisation

Use (CH₃)₃C⁺ and water to draw β-H loss, with both electron-pair arrows and all products.

Hint

Water supplies a pair to H while C–H supplies the alkene π pair.

Worked answer and reasoning

The products are (CH₃)₂C=CH₂ and H₃O⁺. This deprotonation follows prior ionisation in an E1 pathway. It is not the concerted C–Br-breaking E2 event.

11. Contrast E1 with E2

Compare an E1 route from a tertiary bromide in water with an E2 model using a strong base. State the order of bond breaking and the presence or absence of a carbocation.

Hint

Ask whether loss of the leaving group is a separate elementary step.

Worked answer and reasoning

E1 ionises first to a carbocation, then loses β-H. E2 couples base attack, C–H cleavage and C–leaving-group cleavage in one step, without a carbocation intermediate. Conditions and competing routes must still be considered.

12. Choose the attacked atom

For a secondary bromide with a reagent that can act as nucleophile and base, draw a carbon-attack possibility and a β-H-removal possibility.

Hint

The alternatives differ first in where the donated pair goes.

Worked answer and reasoning

Pair donation to saturated carbon with leaving-group departure gives substitution; donation to β-H with C–H electrons forming C=C gives elimination. A reagent label alone does not choose between them.

13. Protonate propene in two ways

For ionic HBr addition without peroxides, draw both formal carbocation connectivities possible after protonating propene.

Hint

H can attach at either alkene carbon, leaving the positive site at the other.

Worked answer and reasoning

Terminal H addition gives secondary CH₃CH⁺CH₃; internal H addition gives primary CH₃CH₂CH₂⁺. The secondary route is favoured in the selected model because its intermediate is more stabilised.

14. Complete bromide capture

Starting from CH₃CH⁺CH₃ and Br⁻, draw pair donation and name the organic product.

Hint

The cationic carbon needs the new bond.

Worked answer and reasoning

A bromide lone pair forms C–Br, giving 2-bromopropane. Carbon becomes tetrahedral and neutral. The HBr addition sequence is protonation followed by capture, not a single arbitrary placement of H and Br.

15. Audit HBr addition

Balance propene + HBr → 2-bromopropane at the formula level and count the local bonds that change.

Hint

A pi bond is consumed while two sigma bonds are made.

Worked answer and reasoning

C₃H₆ + HBr gives C₃H₇Br. The C=C π bond and H–Br bond are consumed overall while C–H and C–Br form; the original C–C sigma bond remains.

16. Recognise a symmetric alkene

Predict the connectivity of ionic HBr addition to ethene. Is there a distinct regioisomeric alternative?

Hint

The two alkene carbons are equivalent by symmetry.

Worked answer and reasoning

The product is bromoethane, CH₃CH₂Br. Exchanging which equivalent carbon receives H gives the same connectivity, so there is no distinct regioisomeric product in this example.

17. Read bromonium opening

Draw a three-membered C–Br⁺–C bridge and Br⁻ approaching a carbon from the opposite side. Show which bridge bond pair returns to Br.

Hint

New C–Br formation opens the corresponding C–Br bridge bond.

Worked answer and reasoning

Bromide attacks carbon from the back of the bridge, and that carbon–bridge-Br pair returns to the bridging Br. This yields two C–Br bonds with anti addition in the representative mechanism.

18. Compare HBr with Br₂

Why should the free-carbocation HBr picture not be copied unchanged when explaining anti Br₂ addition?

Hint

Compare the intermediates, not just the two letters Br in the reagents.

Worked answer and reasoning

The bromonium bridge changes access to the two faces and its backside opening explains anti addition. A freely planar carbocation picture omits this bridge and does not provide the same stereochemical explanation.

19. Identify a false reverse reaction

A student calls every addition the reverse of the corresponding laboratory elimination under the same conditions. Rewrite the claim carefully.

Hint

Distinguish net bond inventory from microscopic path and operating conditions.

Worked answer and reasoning

Addition consumes a pi bond and elimination creates one, so some net transformations can be related. Their useful reagents, mechanisms, equilibrium conditions and competing processes may differ; the names alone do not establish an exact reverse under identical conditions.

20. Explain a complete branch point

A tertiary carbocation is formed in water. Draw both capture leading to alcohol and deprotonation leading to alkene, including proton-transfer products.

Hint

Water can attack carbon or act as base at β-H.

Worked answer and reasoning

Carbon capture gives an oxonium ion, then water deprotonation gives alcohol plus H₃O⁺. β-H removal gives alkene plus H₃O⁺ directly from the cation. Both follow the same early ionisation, so seeing ionisation alone does not settle the product ratio.

Prediction, exploration and explanation

Predict (1 minute). Keep front H1 at twelve o’clock. At which rear-Br angle will H1–Cβ–Cα–Br be 180°? Repeat mentally for H2 at four o’clock.

Explore (3–4 minutes). Choose a reacting front hydrogen and rotate the rear carbon. Find anti alignment without the preset, then use “Align selected H anti” to check. Change the selected H without changing rotation. Does the same conformation remain eligible for that different H?

Explain (2–3 minutes). Draw one aligned Newman view on paper, then the three E2 electron-pair arrows and possible ethene product. Explain what stays unchanged during rotation and why alignment alone does not establish reaction speed or the dominant pathway.

Supplied model and its limits

The explicit substrate is bromoethane, CH₃CH₂Br. View from its beta CH₃ (front) to alpha CH₂Br (rear). Front hydrogens stay fixed at 0°, 120° and 240°; rear Br and its two H atoms rotate together. A ±10° teaching window labels near 180° as near anti; it is not a kinetic threshold or reaction probability. This is a geometry model only: suitable nucleophiles can make primary bromoethane favour substitution instead of elimination.
