Substitution:20 drawing and reasoning problems

Use problems 1–12 as the core route (about 80–100 minutes). Problems 13–20 are optional transfer practice (another 45–65 minutes); select them to target mistakes rather than adding every problem to required contact time. Paper/model drawing is central. All inputs and conventions needed are below; no textbook purchase or external problem set is required.

Local refresher and worked starting example

A nucleophile donates a pair to saturated carbon; a leaving group takes the old bonding pair. SN2 couples these changes and inverts the reacting geometry. SN1 ionises first and then captures a carbocation. Methyl/primary/secondary/tertiary counts carbon neighbours at the reacting carbon. Conditions determine which model applies.

Worked starting example. For OH⁻+CH₃Br, oxygen gains a C–O bond and becomes neutral in CH₃OH; Br receives the C–Br pair as Br⁻. No carbocation appears in this concerted example. A neutral incoming water or ammonia instead acquires positive charge before a later proton transfer.

Draw structures, identify the relevant atom and explain the reason. A name alone is insufficient where a mechanism or argument is requested.

1. Classify four centres

Draw CH₃Br, CH₃CH₂Br, CH₃CH(Br)CH₃ and (CH₃)₃CBr. Classify each carbon bonded to Br and explain your counting rule.

Hint

Count carbon neighbours directly attached to the Br-bearing carbon.

Worked answer and reasoning

The classes are methyl, primary, secondary and tertiary, with zero, one, two and three carbon neighbours respectively. The total carbon count of the whole molecule is not the classification criterion.

2. Same formula, different accessibility

Draw 1-bromobutane and 2-bromobutane. Circle the reaction centre and compare backside crowding under otherwise comparable SN2 conditions.

Hint

Their formulas match; the local carbon environment differs.

Worked answer and reasoning

The terminal reacting carbon in 1-bromobutane is primary and generally less hindered than the secondary carbon in 2-bromobutane. This favours SN2 access for the primary example, without establishing a universal rate ratio.

3. Construct the methyl mechanism

Draw the concerted OH⁻ + CH₃Br SN2 step, showing the leaving pair and net charge.

Hint

Both bond changes occur in one elementary step.

Worked answer and reasoning

OH lone pair → C and C–Br pair → Br produce CH₃OH + Br⁻. Total charge is −1. A methyl carbocation is not an intermediate in this model.

4. Change the incoming group

Assume an SN2 displacement of CH₃Br by I⁻ in a suitable medium. Draw the bond changes and products without claiming the equilibrium position.

Hint

Use iodine as pair donor and keep bromide as the leaving group.

Worked answer and reasoning

The I pair forms C–I while C–Br breaks to Br⁻, producing CH₃I + Br⁻. The same electron-flow pattern applies. Conditions and relative activities are needed to discuss extent of reaction.

5. Build an ether

Assume suitable dry SN2 conditions for CH₃CH₂O⁻ + CH₃CH₂Br. Draw the new bond and both products. Why is the organic product neutral?

Hint

The alkoxide oxygen changes from one bond to two.

Worked answer and reasoning

Products are CH₃CH₂OCH₂CH₃ and Br⁻. Oxygen donates its pair to carbon and becomes a neutral two-bond ether oxygen. Net charge remains −1, now carried by bromide.

6. Let ammonia attack

Draw the first SN2 step of NH₃ + CH₃Br. A proposed answer CH₃NH₂ + Br⁻ is missing something: identify it.

Hint

The attacking nitrogen initially carries three H atoms.

Worked answer and reasoning

The immediate product is CH₃NH₃⁺ + Br⁻. Nitrogen gains a fourth bond and positive charge. Neutral methylamine requires a separate proton-transfer step; it cannot lose H without a proton acceptor.

7. Finish the amine proton transfer

Draw the possible equilibrium proton transfer from CH₃NH₃⁺ to another NH₃. Show both arrows and all products; do not assume quantitative deprotonation.

Hint

Ammonia is the base in this second event.

Worked answer and reasoning

An NH₃ lone pair takes H from methylammonium; the original N–H pair returns to methylamine N. Products are CH₃NH₂ and NH₄⁺. Charge +1 is conserved, and the nitrogen roles differ between attack and deprotonation. This is a valid proton-transfer possibility, not a claim that equilibrium intrinsically favours complete deprotonation. Amounts and reaction conditions, including excess ammonia, affect the resulting mixture.

8. Use the SN2 rate model

For rate=k[RX][Nu], predict the initial-rate factor when [RX] is doubled and [Nu] tripled at fixed temperature and solvent.

Hint

Multiply the independent concentration factors.

Worked answer and reasoning

The rate increases sixfold in the supplied simple model. The calculation describes initial kinetics and does not imply a sixfold equilibrium conversion or yield.

9. Hold the rate constant

In the same rate model, [RX] is halved while [Nu] is doubled. Predict the rate factor and state the assumptions.

Hint

One factor offsets the other if k is unchanged.

Worked answer and reasoning

The predicted initial rate is unchanged: 0.5×2=1. Temperature, solvent and the applicable mechanism must remain comparable; changing those can change k or the mechanism.

10. Reject an obstructed pathway

Compare CH₃CH₂Br and (CH₃)₃CBr for direct backside substitution by the same nucleophile. Why should a tertiary substrate not be assigned SN2 merely because the nucleophile is strong?

Hint

Draw the carbon groups around the reaction centre.

Worked answer and reasoning

Three carbon substituents strongly obstruct backside approach at the tertiary centre. A strong donor cannot remove this geometric requirement; elimination or a stepwise route may compete depending on conditions. Primary ethyl is much more accessible.

11. Begin a tertiary SN1 route

For (CH₃)₃CBr in an ionising aqueous medium, draw C–Br heterolysis and label the product charges.

Hint

The departing group receives the entire C–Br pair.

Worked answer and reasoning

Products are (CH₃)₃C⁺ and Br⁻. The tertiary carbocation is more stabilised than a methyl cation, and the medium can stabilise separated charge. Ionisation does not yet specify the final capture or elimination outcome.

12. Capture with water

Draw H₂O attack on (CH₃)₃C⁺. Count oxygen bonds in the immediate product.

Hint

Use a water lone pair, not a hydrogen, to make the new bond.

Worked answer and reasoning

The oxonium product is (CH₃)₃COH₂⁺. Oxygen has three bonds and charge +1. Writing the neutral alcohol at this stage would silently remove a proton.

Note

Optional transfer starts here. Choose problems that address your own gaps; the core route remains1–12.

13. Obtain neutral alcohol

Use H₂O to deprotonate (CH₃)₃COH₂⁺. Include the species made from the base.

Hint

Water becomes hydronium after accepting H⁺.

Worked answer and reasoning

Products are (CH₃)₃COH and H₃O⁺. The base pair forms O–H and the original oxonium O–H pair returns to its oxygen. The net charge is +1 before and after.

14. Balance the overall aqueous route

Combine tertiary ionisation, water capture and water deprotonation into one net equation starting from (CH₃)₃CBr. Include bromide.

Hint

Two waters appear in the explicit overall accounting.

Worked answer and reasoning

(CH₃)₃CBr + 2 H₂O → (CH₃)₃COH + H₃O⁺ + Br⁻. This represents the selected substitution path; it does not exclude competing elimination. Atoms and overall zero charge balance.

15. Assign a particular inversion

Start with (S)-2-bromobutane and assume stereospecific SN2 replacement of Br by OH. Assign the product configuration by checking priorities rather than just changing the letter.

Hint

For each structure, rank the heteroatom group, ethyl, methyl and H.

Worked answer and reasoning

Both Br and OH are highest priority in their respective structures; ethyl outranks methyl and H is lowest. Backside geometric inversion therefore gives (R)-butan-2-ol in this particular example. A different replacement could change the priority order and the letter relationship.

16. Explain two-face access

A planar carbocation reacts in an achiral medium to form a stereocentre. Explain why both configurations may form and why an exactly 50:50 mixture is not guaranteed in every SN1 reaction.

Hint

Distinguish an isolated planar model from an actual ion pair and environment.

Worked answer and reasoning

Both faces of a planar intermediate may be accessible. Nearby leaving-group ions or other environmental constraints can bias capture, so the geometric argument supports both-face reaction without promising an exact racemic ratio in every experiment.

17. Activate an alcohol

Draw CH₃CH₂OH protonation by H₃O⁺ and name the better departing group after protonation. Do not assume a primary carbocation forms.

Hint

Change the oxygen protonation state before discussing departure.

Worked answer and reasoning

The alcohol gives CH₃CH₂OH₂⁺ plus H₂O. Neutral water is a better leaving group than hydroxide. That activation allows suitable substitution pathways, but a primary substrate still should not be forced into an unsupported SN1 carbocation model.

18. Keep solvent claims controlled

A student predicts identical rates for CH₃Br + OH⁻ in every solvent because the two reactant formulas are unchanged. Give a chemically justified objection.

Hint

The medium can alter ion stabilisation and donor availability.

Worked answer and reasoning

Solvation changes the energies and effective reactivity of the charged nucleophile and transition state. Keeping formulas the same does not keep k unchanged. State solvent and temperature before transferring rate comparisons between experiments.

19. Repair an atom-loss error

A proposed tertiary-water mechanism changes (CH₃)₃COH₂⁺ directly to (CH₃)₃COH and writes no other species. Add a valid missing step.

Hint

Supply a base and show where the departing proton goes.

Worked answer and reasoning

Another water takes H⁺ while the oxonium O–H pair returns to O, producing alcohol and H₃O⁺. The corrected step conserves H atoms and charge +1.

20. Name the competing route

From (CH₃)₃C⁺ in water, draw removal of a β-H and the resulting alkene. How does this differ from water attack at carbon?

Hint

The C–H pair can form a C=C bond to the cationic centre.

Worked answer and reasoning

Water acting as base removes β-H, giving 2-methylpropene and hydronium through E1. Water acting as nucleophile at carbon instead starts the alcohol-forming SN1 path. The common intermediate permits competing outcomes.
