# First teaching draft: implementation and review

19 November 2026. Scope: the new 7.5-credit Organic Chemistry 1, with a complete
five-week teaching route and a deliberately small R/Rmd component.

## Revised course and checks

Restored the source scaffold's `history: false`. Browser checks enter three
representative lectures through the homepage, change slides several times,
verify no additional history entries, and return home with one Back action.
All 15 rendered decks also expose the expected history setting.

Each lecture has a concrete 45-minute budget including named drawing/check
pauses. Approximately 40 seconds per slide remains guidance; counts vary from
40 to 59 according to the topic. The revised course has 784 slides, including
446 meaningful visual slides (56.9% overall); every deck exceeds 50%.

The textbook-independent bank covers all 25 study days: 300 unit problems
and 120 mixed-day problems, each with supplied inputs, a hint and a reasoned
answer. Ninety formative self-check questions and eight R activities remain
separate. The local reference toolkit supplies drawing conventions, electron
counting, stereochemical rules, common shorthand and numerical/spectral methods.
The build contains 89 student pages and 340 original SVGs plus two R plots,
alongside the local SVG workbenches generated by the 25 interactive demos.

Three workers revised separate topic groups and independently reviewed each
other's new paper banks. Review led to explicit stereochemical frameworks,
clarification that butane has no configurational stereoisomers, a supplied
oxidation-state convention and an explicit equilibrium qualification for
ammonia/alkylammonium proton transfer. Existing R tasks were restored after
an editing regression and all complete R examples pass in a clean project.

`make figures`, `make render` and `make check` complete successfully. The local
browser check covers all 784 slides with every figure loaded: no missing
images or content extending beyond the canvas. All referenced built assets
match their sources. Representative acid–base, stereochemistry and spectral
slides were visually inspected. Full counts and test results are in the
adjacent slide, practice, layout and navigation inventories.

## Scientific review and corrections

After the scaffold and acid–base pilot, three parallel workers built separate
topic groups. Each group received an independent read-only scientific review
from another worker. Parent review included selected original SVGs and browser
screenshots. These are author/cross-unit checks, not departmental approval.

Corrections included unambiguous skeletal terminal atoms, actual lone-pair dots
in the early oxygen-charge diagram, chair-flip geometry, resonance versus
equilibrium arrows, carbocation face approaches, protonated carbonyl labels,
activation-barrier baseline, carbon NMR label spacing, and the dependency of
theoretical yield on starting amounts rather than isolated mass.

Review checked lactic-acid Fischer priorities/configuration, meso relationships,
E/Z and Newman comparisons, ring flips, elementary reaction accounting,
amide/amine and aromatic-nitrogen reasoning, and the simulated ester spectral
assignments. Teaching models explicitly label their assumptions and provenance.
